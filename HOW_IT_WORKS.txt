HOW IT WORKS - Night Tide
JIN2 · @jin_22ee

The whole artwork is one Python file. Every cell of the screen asks
"what part of the sea am I looking at, and how much light does it catch?"
and the answer becomes a letter and a colour.

1. The camera
   We stand on the beach at night, looking out to sea. Each cell below the
   horizon is turned into a point on the water: rows near the top are far
   away, rows near the bottom are close. The last rows are sand.

2. The sea surface
   Ten swells of different sizes and directions are added together.
   They are Gerstner waves: the water moves in small circles, so crests
   come out sharp and troughs wide. Long waves travel faster than short
   ones, like real deep water. Waves too small to see from far away are
   faded out, so the distance does not turn into noise.

3. Light on the water
   There is no moon in the sky. For each cell the code works out which
   way the water is tilted and what part of the night sky it mirrors.
   Water seen at a low angle reflects more (the Fresnel effect), so the
   far sea glows and the near sea stays dark. Lit crests can catch the
   moon for a moment and flash silver.

4. The breakers
   Every 2.5 seconds a new wave comes in, and no two are the same:
   different size, angle, bend and timing. Each one rolls in as a swell,
   stands up with a bright lip, breaks into round white lumps with a
   little spray, then rushes in as white water that tears into lace.
   Small waves break further out, big ones close to the shore, so white
   lines show at more than one depth.

5. On the sand
   The white water runs up the beach as a thin sheet with a scalloped
   edge, slides back, and leaves a line of foam that slowly fades.
   Far out, swell tops tip over into small whitecaps now and then.

6. From light to letters
   Darker water uses light letters (: ; 1 i), brighter water uses denser
   ones (0 8 B $ #), and six blues run from deep navy to silver.
   Foam is white, glitter is silver (* + ^ .).
   Each frame fades into the next and a soft glow is added on top.

Things you can try (for yourself)
   SWELLS          the waves on the open sea
   PERIOD, RISE    how often breakers come and how tall they stand
   Z_BREAK         how close to the shore they break
   HORIZON, EYE    where the horizon sits and how high you stand
   TAG_COLOR       the colours
